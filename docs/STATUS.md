## Differences from the paper

* **Symmetric functions.** `Symm = MvPolynomial ℕ ℚ` uses coordinate `j` for the formal
  elementary generator `e_{j+1}`. `chromSymInf` is the canonical elementary expansion of the
  CSF in `|V|` variables. `stable_epositive` proves that one elementary polynomial works in
  every alphabet; `chromSymInf_epositive` proves coefficientwise nonnegativity and identifies
  every finite specialization. `specializeSymm_jointly_injective` shows that agreement in
  all alphabets is equality in `Symm`.
* **Lemma `tableau-mult`.** `MarkedNUIG.comp` glues arbitrary marked NUIGs using their word
  realizations. `Word.comp_overlap`, `comp_cover`, and `adj_comp` prove that exactly the
  boundary vertices are identified, every vertex comes from a factor, and the edges are
  exactly the inherited edges. `MarkedNUIG.tableau_mult` proves the graph's directly defined
  Hikita matrix is the product of the two Hikita matrices.
* **Tableaux as column assignments.** A tableau built from `T_j` is recorded by the columns of its
  vertices (`IsBuild`). `isBuild_of_hwt_ne_zero` shows that assignments of nonzero weight are
  tableaux, so the sums over all assignments in `Hikita.lean` are sums over tableaux.
* **Probabilities and shapes.** `hprob` is the paper's product of transition probabilities.
  `weight_pr` telescopes the column-height potential; `row_potential` identifies this potential
  with `∏ r, λ_r! e_{λ_r}`. `weight_pr_rows` is Equation `weight-pr`, including a nonempty initial
  tableau. `hikita_probability_rows` states Hikita's theorem for any row-length description
  whose column heights are those of the tableau; `hikita_probability_tableaux` uses column
  heights directly.
* **Examples.** `Examples.lean` checks the actual graphs, word realizations, CSF polynomials
  in every alphabet, and the two larger tableau products. `ExamplesMatrices.lean` checks the
  displayed `3 × 3` matrices `D₂M₁`, `(D₂M₁)²`, and `U₁`. The CSF calculations use weighted
  deletion-contraction certificates generated by `scripts/generate_examples.py`. Sympy only
  proposes polynomial expressions: Lean verifies every recurrence and identity. No
  `native_decide` or additional computational axiom is used. Drawings are not formal objects.
* **Arc models.** A proper circular-arc model consists of closed arcs `[s, s + ℓ]` of `ℝ/ℤ` with
  `0 ≤ ℓ < 1`, none containing another (`PCAModel`).
